## What we did

Opened Signboard's scope beyond sign and print shops, without building for any other trade ([#54](https://github.com/signboard-works/signboard/issues/54)). Stuart wanted to keep the options open, with no other trade in mind.

[ADR 0049](../engineering/decisions/0049-scope-made-to-order-businesses.md) records the decision. It is built for sign and print, and open to any made-to-order business:

- the model's names are trade-neutral;
- trade-specific features, such as proofs and site visits, are capabilities a business switches on;
- each trade's configuration is a starter pack, and sign and print is the only one;
- the pitch, the vision and the design partner shop stay sign and print.

A fresh-context audit of the docs then found where the model leaked the trade, and this pull request fixes them:

- **Glossary.** The business, work centre, pricing method and proof are now defined in general. Material, unit of measure, site visit, capability and starter pack are new terms. The trade terms now sit under "sign and print".
- **Pricing.** Pricing methods are general. A measured quantity takes a unit of measure, and area and length are the sign and print cases. Units of measure become a platform-wide collection in the database that operations users manage, not a fixed list in code (ADR 0036). A quote line has no fixed width and height.
- **Roles and fields.** The Installer role is now **Field staff**. ADR 0036's appointment example uses `visitDate` and `visitTime`, not `installDate` and `installTime`.
- **Jobs and vehicles.** Vehicle details are no longer a fixed part of jobs, and no rule may assume a particular work centre exists.
- **Wording.** ADR 0006 now says "business" rather than "shop". The vision, home page, ADRs 0004, 0010 and 0022, the PRD template and several PRDs are reworded.
- **Instructions.** `AGENTS.md` and CodeRabbit's instructions gain the trade-neutral naming rule.

The audit also found example paths in ADRs 0016 and 0017 that left out the module segment ADR 0020 requires, such as `/api/v1/jobs` for `/api/v1/production/jobs`. Stuart asked for them to be fixed in the same pull request ([#56](https://github.com/signboard-works/signboard/issues/56)).

Then Stuart reviewed the first of the draft ADRs from 2026-10-11 ([#36](https://github.com/signboard-works/signboard/issues/36)) and accepted Claude's recommendations on all nine questions. The draft was split in two:

- [ADR 0043](../engineering/decisions/0043-backups-and-recovery.md), backups and disaster recovery:
    - recovery targets as drafted;
    - before the first paying business, locked copies in a separate backup account in Melbourne, kept for 35 days, with no long-term archives;
    - Vault Lock in governance mode, then compliance mode after a month without changes;
    - an automated restore test every month from before the first paying business, and a scripted quarterly drill from the first paying business.
- [ADR 0050](../engineering/decisions/0050-database-migrations.md), database migrations:
    - expand then contract;
    - migrations run as their own step before the deploy;
    - fixed forward, never rolled back.

[ADR 0030](../engineering/decisions/0030-hosting-aws.md) gains the backup account, the Melbourne exception (still in Australia) and the extra A$10 a month.

Stuart then accepted the recommendations for the privacy draft ([#37](https://github.com/signboard-works/signboard/issues/37)), and it too was split:

- [ADR 0044](../engineering/decisions/0044-privacy-and-data-residency.md), privacy and data residency:
    - every business is treated as covered by the Australian Privacy Principles;
    - PostHog is the one residency exception, and Stripe is named as the next expected one;
    - the policy and terms are drafted from a template and reviewed by a lawyer.
- [ADR 0051](../engineering/decisions/0051-data-export-and-leaving.md), data export and leaving:
    - export at any time;
    - one export and import tool;
    - a closing business is read-only for 30 days, with reminders and the option to cancel, then deleted.

Closing a user account changed from the draft. The person's name now stays on each business's records, shown as "Jane Doe (Deactivated)", which Stuart chose after the way Confluence shows former users, until the business chooses to de-identify them. The draft replaced it with "Former user" everywhere.

On Stuart's question about people who return, a person removed from a business and invited back keeps their user ID, so their history links up again. A person who closed their own account and signs up again is a new user, with nothing linking the two. Closing an account now takes 30 days, and signing in during that time cancels it.

Then [ADR 0045](../engineering/decisions/0045-search.md), search ([#38](https://github.com/signboard-works/signboard/issues/38)), was accepted with Claude's recommendations. Two changed from the draft:

- **Hiding fields.** Search documents are kept per object and field group, rather than one shared document plus a "staff-only" one. Staff roles differ, so under the draft a production user could have found a job by its price.
- **Keeping it current.** An object's own fields are indexed in the save transaction by the owning module. Text copied from related objects is re-indexed by an outbox job. The draft built everything in database triggers in the same transaction.

Descriptions also get English stemming. A unique PO number opens its object, as a full ID does. "See all" hands a search to the grid.

[ADR 0046](../engineering/decisions/0046-entitlements.md), entitlements ([#39](https://github.com/signboard-works/signboard/issues/39)), was accepted with Claude's recommendations. Two things changed from the draft:

- **Reading is never gated.** The draft both left the fields of features outside the plan out of responses and promised that a downgraded feature stays visible, read-only. Now entitlements gate using a feature, never reading the business's own data.
- **The core workflow is never gated**, accounting sync included. The draft used accounting sync as its example of a gated feature.

ADR 0029 gains `limit_reached`, and ADRs 0033 and 0038 now link to ADR 0046.

[ADR 0047](../engineering/decisions/0047-sign-up-plans-and-billing.md), sign-up, plans and billing ([#40](https://github.com/signboard-works/signboard/issues/40)), was accepted with Claude's recommendations:

- **One plan, Standard, per business with unlimited users**, at A$49 a month or A$490 a year, so about ten businesses paying monthly, or twelve paying yearly, cover the running costs. The draft left the price open.
- **No free plan**; self-hosting is the free option, and the hosted service has a 30-day trial.
- **Manual invoices and bank transfer** at launch, Stripe Billing later, and no merchant of record. Stuart asked what a merchant of record is. It is the company that legally sells to the customer and handles the sales tax; it costs about twice Stripe's fees, and matters mostly for consumer sales and for the United States.
- **14 days past due, then read-only**, with three reminders.
- **Trials that are never paid for, other than complimentary ones, are closed** 90 days after they end, with notice and ADR 0051's 30-day closure. Businesses that have paid are still never deleted for non-payment.
- **Who operates the hosted service**, a sole trader or a company, and its GST registration, is not an ADR question. It is now a task for Stuart and an accountant ([#59](https://github.com/signboard-works/signboard/issues/59)).

ADR 0029 gains `account_read_only`, the data model registers the `SUB` prefix, and ADRs 0004 and 0051 link to ADR 0047.

## Decisions

- Signboard's model is trade-neutral; sign and print stays the pitch and the only starter pack, by Stuart ([ADR 0049](../engineering/decisions/0049-scope-made-to-order-businesses.md)).
- The name Signboard stays, by Stuart.
- Backups, recovery targets and restore testing as in [ADR 0043](../engineering/decisions/0043-backups-and-recovery.md), and migrations split into [ADR 0050](../engineering/decisions/0050-database-migrations.md), by Stuart.
- Former users stay named on businesses' records as "(Deactivated)", and privacy and data export are split into [ADR 0044](../engineering/decisions/0044-privacy-and-data-residency.md) and [ADR 0051](../engineering/decisions/0051-data-export-and-leaving.md), by Stuart.
- Search in PostgreSQL, indexed per field group, as in [ADR 0045](../engineering/decisions/0045-search.md), by Stuart.
- Entitlements gate using features, never reading data, and never the core workflow, as in [ADR 0046](../engineering/decisions/0046-entitlements.md), by Stuart.
- One plan per business with unlimited users, A$49 a month, manual invoicing at launch, and unpaid trials closed after 90 days, as in [ADR 0047](../engineering/decisions/0047-sign-up-plans-and-billing.md), by Stuart.
- Units of measure are a database collection configured by operations users, by Stuart ([ADR 0036](../engineering/decisions/0036-money-tax-and-time.md#quantities)).

## What the AI got wrong or was overruled on

- **A stalled wait on CodeRabbit.** The loop waiting to merge [#53](https://github.com/signboard-works/signboard/pull/53) waited for CodeRabbit's summary review of the last commit. CodeRabbit had finished, but answered only in the comment threads, so the loop never ended. It kept waiting for about 90 minutes, until Stuart asked why the pull request had stalled. The wait now checks that CodeRabbit's status check has passed and that every thread is resolved or answered.
- **Clashing terms.** The first draft of ADR 0049 suggested "process or stage" as model terms, although the docs already use **work centre**. The audit caught it and the draft now says work centre.
- **Wrong example paths.** ADRs 0016 and 0017 used `/api/v1/jobs`, without the module segment, after ADR 0020 had settled on `/api/v1/{module}/{collection}`. The AI wrote both examples and did not update them when that rule was made.
- **Units in code.** CodeRabbit pointed out that the pricing engine let a business configure units such as weight, while ADR 0036 kept a fixed list of units in code. The AI resolved it by keeping the list in code. Stuart overruled that: units of measure are a collection in the database that operations users configure.
- **Arguing both sides of a split.** On 2026-10-11 the AI defended keeping migrations inside the backups ADR when CodeRabbit asked for a split. Asked for a recommendation today, it recommended the split. The first answer defended the draft instead of judging it.
- **An erasure log that a restore would lose.** CodeRabbit asked where the erasure log lives. As drafted, it lived only in the database, so restoring a snapshot from before an erasure would also lose the entries needed to repeat it. It is now an Object Lock bucket in the Melbourne backup account, which every restore reads. CodeRabbit then found a second gap: the first fix wrote the entry after the erasure and copied it to Melbourne later, so losing Sydney in between would lose it. Each entry is now written to Melbourne and confirmed before anything is erased. CodeRabbit also found that ADR 0008 still said migrations were applied automatically on upgrade. It now points to ADR 0050.
- **A made-up citation.** In reply to CodeRabbit's grammar note on "the deploy" as a noun, the AI wrote that ADRs 0015 and 0028 used it. They don't. It checked afterwards and corrected the reply in the thread. The noun is used elsewhere, such as in ADR 0034. Claims made in a review reply need checking like any other claim.
- **A search leak in the draft.** The ADR 0045 draft treated staff as one audience, with a single staff-only search document. Production staff cannot see prices, so they could have found jobs by price. The AI caught it on re-reading the draft for Stuart's review, a day after writing it.
- **A contradiction in the entitlements draft.** ADR 0046 as drafted hid the fields of features outside a business's plan and also promised that a downgraded feature stays visible, read-only. Under that draft, a downgrade would have made the business's own data disappear from the API. The AI found it while preparing the review.
- **Gaps in the billing draft.** The ADR 0047 draft promised that nothing is deleted for non-payment, so every abandoned trial would have been kept, and paid for, indefinitely. It also had a `cancelled` status that duplicated ADR 0051's closure without linking to it, and it quoted the launch running costs (A$340) rather than the costs once paying businesses arrive (A$475). The AI found all three while preparing the review.
- **An old inconsistency.** The audit also found that the quotes PRD and open question 1 left out length as a pricing method, although the pricing engine and the glossary include it. Both are fixed.

## Lessons

- A naming pass is cheap only while there is no code. A fresh-context audit found more than a keyword search would have: the Installer role, the `installDate` example and the fixed list of pricing methods were all model decisions hidden in ordinary sentences.
- Wait on the state that matters (a check passing, threads answered), not on the shape of a bot's message.
