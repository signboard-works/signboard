## Decisions

- Zensical over Material for MkDocs and GitBook, by Stuart ([ADR 0002](../engineering/decisions/0002-docs-as-code.md)). Pinned to an exact version while it is pre-1.0.
- Editions apply only to the hosted service; self-hosted installations get every feature. The entire product is open source, with no open core. By Stuart; to be recorded in the entitlements ADR.
- Branching, CI and releases ([ADR 0028](../engineering/decisions/0028-branching-ci-and-releases.md)), by Stuart: trunk-based development with squash merge, Conventional Commits pull request titles, versioned releases from the start with release-please, and Dependabot, all as Claude recommended. Stuart asked how testing works before merge without a `develop` branch; Claude's answer (checks on the pull request, preview environments, the full suite on `main`, a merge queue once there are contributors) went into the ADR.
- API errors, idempotency and versioning ([ADR 0029](../engineering/decisions/0029-api-errors-idempotency-versioning.md)), by Stuart, all as Claude recommended. Stuart shared the SoftwareOne Marketplace API's error format for comparison and preferred ours: that format is ASP.NET Core's default, whose `type` links to RFC 9110's definition of the status code rather than identifying the error.
- Hosting on AWS in Sydney ([ADR 0030](../engineering/decisions/0030-hosting-aws.md)), by Stuart: CDK in C#, three accounts, a single-zone database at launch moving to Multi-AZ before the first paying business. The observability backend (CloudWatch and X-Ray) was decided inside this ADR on Claude's recommendation, and ADR 0025 updated to match.
- Concurrency ([ADR 0031](../engineering/decisions/0031-concurrency.md)), by Stuart, as Claude recommended: `If-Match` required on actions too, `If-Match: *` allowed for integrations, automatic reapply of non-overlapping edits, `revision` in the JSON, `304` from the start, and a presence indicator considered with live updates.
- Events, webhooks and live updates ([ADR 0032](../engineering/decisions/0032-events-webhooks-live-updates.md)), by Stuart, as Claude recommended: two kinds of event, thin notifications, a public SSE stream, the editing banner, Standard Webhooks with the full object, webhooks for business accounts only at launch, and 7-day retention.
- Feature flags ([ADR 0033](../engineering/decisions/0033-feature-flags.md)), by Stuart, as Claude recommended: Microsoft.FeatureManagement behind our own interface, flags declared in code with a remove-by date enforced by CI, only operations users switch flags, and a flagged-off feature does not exist in the API.
- Background work ([ADR 0034](../engineering/decisions/0034-background-work.md)), by Stuart, as Claude recommended: our own PostgreSQL job queue, enqueued in the same transaction, tenant context on every job, per-business fairness and per-key ordering, failed jobs visible in the operations app, AWS notifications through SQS.
- Retries and resilience ([ADR 0035](../engineering/decisions/0035-retries-and-resilience.md)), by Stuart, as Claude recommended: no outside calls inside API requests, two layers of retries never stacked, only temporary failures and safe-to-repeat operations, circuit breakers and rate limiters per destination, and fault-injection tests.
- Money, tax, time and quantities ([ADR 0036](../engineering/decisions/0036-money-tax-and-time.md)), by Stuart: amounts as plain JSON numbers rather than strings ("numbers are numbers"), and otherwise as Claude recommended: `decimal` and `numeric` throughout, one currency per business, half-away-from-zero rounding, tax per line, rates fixed once issued, NodaTime with an injected clock, the business's time zone for display, and a unit on every quantity.
- JSON types ([ADR 0029](../engineering/decisions/0029-api-errors-idempotency-versioning.md#json-types)), by Stuart, as Claude recommended: percentages in `…Percent` fields, `…At` and `…Date` names, decimal hours, every visible field always present with `null` for no value, and no `""` or `0` standing in for nothing.
- Localisation ([ADR 0037](../engineering/decisions/0037-localisation.md)), by Stuart, as Claude recommended: English only at launch with every string externalised, Australian English as the source, i18next, formats from the business's locale, a document language per customer, E.164 phone numbers and structured addresses, and a pseudo-locale smoke run on pull requests. Dates are never shown in a locale-dependent numeric format, with ISO 8601 as Stuart's exception.
- Rate limiting ([ADR 0038](../engineering/decisions/0038-rate-limiting.md)), by Stuart, as Claude recommended: three layers, approximate in-memory fair-use limits without Redis, growing delays and no lockouts, a daily email limit of 1,000 per business on the hosted service, `RateLimit` headers once they are an RFC, and self-hosters able to turn off only the fair-use limits.
- Four tabs, with the API reference as its own tab for integrators rather than under Engineering, by Stuart on Claude's recommendation.

## What the AI got wrong or was overruled on

- Stuart found the nav hard to follow and preferred GitBook's look. While checking options, Claude found that Material for MkDocs, which it had recommended the day before for publishing, is in maintenance mode with support ending on 5 November 2026. It had not checked before recommending it.
- The first theme pass had an empty header row on every document's status table, blue links next to an orange highlight, and an orange too pale for WCAG AA contrast. The screenshots caught all three before merge.
- Claude recommended running the full Playwright suite on every pull request "while it's small", splitting it later. Stuart overruled it: "let's do it right from the start; temporary often becomes permanent." End-to-end tests are tiered from day one.
- Claude's first hosting estimate, "roughly A$100–200 a month", covered production only, from memory. Priced properly, production alone is about A$204 a month including GST, and with staging and preview environments the total is about A$324 at launch and A$452 before paying businesses. Stuart accepted the higher figure.
- Claude recommended decimal strings for amounts "so JavaScript clients can't lose precision". Asked how critical that was, it corrected itself: values of up to 15 significant digits survive a JavaScript number exactly, and the real risk is floating-point arithmetic in the client, which strings only discourage. Stuart chose numbers.
- Claude listed localisation and rate limiting as one item, though they have nothing in common. Stuart questioned it, and they became two ADRs.
- Claude's list of missing ADRs left out feature flags and edition entitlements.
- Claude added today's work to the 2026-10-10 journal entry instead of starting a new one for 2026-10-11. Stuart spotted it.

## Lessons

- "Check a library's current state" applies to tools as well as libraries. Check the support status of anything recommended, not just whether it works today.
- Look at the visual result before calling a visual change done. Headless screenshots took seconds and found three problems.
- Quote costs for the whole setup (production, staging, previews, tax and exchange rate), not just production, and price from the provider's price list rather than memory.
- "For now, until it gets slow" is a plan to never do it. Decide the end state up front when the cost of doing so is small.
- When explaining a recommendation, lead with its weakness as well as its strengths. Explaining the business-locale choice exposed the date-order problem the draft had missed.
- Give the real size of a risk, not the reason that sounds best. "Can't lose precision" was the persuasive argument for strings, and the wrong one.
- One journal file per day worked. Check the date before adding to an entry.
